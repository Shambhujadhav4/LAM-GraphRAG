### Abstract
```text
Complex enterprise documents—such as financial filings, clinical study protocols, engineering blueprints, and legal contracts—encode critical domain knowledge across multi-page visual layouts, 2D tabular grids, and infographics. While Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) mitigates large language model hallucinations, conventional architectures rely on 1D text chunking and dense vector similarity. This design shears tabular records, discards visual coordinates, and obscures the provenance chain linking generated claims back to original document regions. Guided by the PRISMA 2020 systematic review methodology, this paper establishes a foundational investigation into spatial evidence traceability across multimodal document intelligence. From an initial automated harvest of 540 candidate records across major scientific registries (IEEE Xplore, ACM DL, ACL Anthology, Scopus, arXiv) spanning 2017–2026, 532 deduplicated unique records underwent standardized title/abstract double-screening (503/532 = 94.55% raw agreement, Cohen's κ = 0.81), yielding 86 full-text candidate articles. Following rigorous double-appraisal against operational exclusion criteria (82/86 = 95.35% raw agreement, κ = 0.90), 50 research sources were synthesized, stratified into 23 core primary empirical systems and 27 supporting literature sources (benchmarks, surveys, foundational baselines, guidelines). We formulate a five-stage Evidence Lifecycle Framework (S1–S5) to systematically map where information loss occurs and how it can be mitigated. We formalize operational tabular integrity metrics (Cell Integrity Ratio and Row Continuity Ratios), mathematically contrast algebraic multi-hop matrix reachability formulations with unconstrained stochastic graph walks, and decouple benchmark evaluation across four explicit dimensions: text retrieval, page-level grounding, region-level coordinate localization, and claim attribution. Finally, we synthesize architectural design principles into Layout-Aware Multimodal GraphRAG (LAM-GraphRAG), outlining an open research agenda for verifiable, provenance-grounded multimodal document understanding.
```
